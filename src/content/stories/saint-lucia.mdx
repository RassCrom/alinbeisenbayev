About twenty countries are named after people. America takes its name from Amerigo Vespucci, Colombia from Christopher Columbus, Bolivia from Simón Bolívar, Uzbekistan, by one account, from Özbeg Khan. Some of these people are famous everywhere, others only at home. They have one thing in common: all of them are men. Except one.

Can you name it? I couldn't have, to be honest, until I came across her story.

## A small island in the Caribbean

The continent is the easy part. Many countries in the Americas carry the name of a European who supposedly "discovered" them. The only country named after a woman is Saint Lucia, a small island in the southern Caribbean.

It is roughly the size of Madrid: 617 km² against the city's 604. The 2022 census counted about 179,000 people, most of them on the coast and around the capital, Castries, because the interior is steep, forested volcanic ridges that climb to Mount Gimie at 950 m.

Saint Lucia became independent from the United Kingdom in 1979 but kept the monarchy. Its head of state is King Charles III.

## The island of iguanas

The island's history starts long before any European arrived. The Arawak people and later the Kalinago (historically called the Carib) lived there first. They called it Iouanalao and Hewanorra, names usually translated as "the land where the iguana is found", after the large iguana population. The second name is still in daily use: the island's main international airport is Hewanorra International.

So how did it become Saint Lucia? And why after a woman who lived some 1,200 years before Europeans reached the Caribbean?

## Saint Lucy and 13 December

It comes down to Christianity. Lucia of Syracuse was a Christian in the Roman Empire who was killed in 304, during the persecution under Diocletian, for refusing to renounce her faith. She is one of the best-known virgin martyrs, and her feast day is 13 December.

That date is the reason for the name. Europeans tied the island's discovery, or at least its naming, to Saint Lucy's day. One story links it to French sailors shipwrecked on the island; another says Spanish explorers sighted it on that date. Neither account is well documented, but the date stuck: 13 December is still Saint Lucia's National Day.

That is how a Caribbean island ended up named after a woman who lived more than a thousand years earlier, and the only country in the world to carry a woman's name.
