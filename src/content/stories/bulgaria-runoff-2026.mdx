On 1 November 2026 Bulgaria held a presidential run-off, and President Iliana Iotova beat her challenger, Andrey Gyurov. According to the official results, Iotova won 57.6% of the vote to Gyurov's 39.5%. She is the first woman elected president of Bulgaria.

## Why there was a second round

To win outright in the first round on 25 October, a candidate needed more than half of the vote. Nobody came close: Iotova led with 34.8%, ahead of Gyurov with 31.9%, so the two met again a week later.

## Two directions for the country

Iotova is backed by the governing Progressive Bulgaria party and its founder, Prime Minister Rumen Radev, who held the presidency before her. She favours dialogue with Russia and opposes military aid to Ukraine.

Gyurov is backed by the pro-European opposition parties. He argued for closer ties with the EU and NATO and for continued support to Ukraine.

Iotova carried 28 of the 31 electoral regions, about 1.09 million votes against Gyurov's 750,000. Two of Gyurov's three regions are in the capital: he took Sofia's 23rd region with 59.9% and the 24th with 56.2%, while Iotova held the third Sofia region by under four points. His other win was Kardzhali in the south, where only 13% of registered voters turned out.

Plovdiv city, the second-largest city, also stayed close (52.1% for Iotova). Outside Sofia and Plovdiv her lead was wide: in 22 of the remaining 26 regions she won by 25 points or more.

## The vote abroad

Bulgarians abroad went the other way. About 108,000 of them voted, and 56.4% chose Gyurov. That was nowhere near enough to change the result, but it matches the capital, the other place where Gyurov came first.

About 3% of voters in Bulgaria picked the ballot's "I support no one" option.
